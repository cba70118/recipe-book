Cheap "Paella"

Easy · 40 min Serves 8
Zatarain's
dinnercajunporkseafoodweeknightone-panfamily-dinnerskilletdutch-oven
+
Why This Works: Browning the sausage first renders fat that you then use to saute the onion, building a fond that adds depth to what is essentially a box rice mix. Adding the shrimp and peas at the very end keeps the shrimp from turning rubbery and the peas from going army-green. The Zatarain's yellow rice handles the saffron-turmeric flavor so you get paella vibes without the price tag.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups water
  • 1/2 package Cajun style smoked sausage, halved lengthwise and sliced 1/4-inch thick
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes, undrained
  • 1/2 cup chopped onion
  • 1 tsp parsley flakes
  • 1 package Zatarain's Yellow Rice Mix
  • 1 lb large shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 1 cup frozen peas

Steps

  1. Brown the sliced sausage in a large skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Remove and set aside.
  2. Sauté the onion in the same pan using the rendered sausage fat until softened, about 3-5 minutes.
  3. Add water and diced tomatoes (undrained). Bring to a boil.
  4. Stir in the Zatarain's Yellow Rice Mix. Return the sausage to the pan.
  5. Reduce heat to low, cover, and simmer for about 20 minutes until rice is nearly tender.
  6. Stir in shrimp and frozen peas. Cover and cook 5-7 minutes until shrimp are pink and cooked through.
  7. Remove from heat, sprinkle with parsley flakes, fluff with a fork, and serve.
Notes: Prep: 10 min, Cook: 30 min. Serves 8. Quick weeknight crowd-feeder.

Classic Genovese Basil Pesto

Easy · 20 min About 1 cup (enough for 1 lb pasta)
Adapted from Serious Eats / J. Kenji López-Alt
dinnerlunchitalianvegetarianweeknightmake-aheadbatch-cookingsummerdate-nightfood-processorskillet
+
Why This Works: Toasting the pine nuts deepens their flavor and drives off moisture so the pesto stays thick. Folding the cheese in by hand instead of processing it avoids the heat and friction that would turn the Parmesan gummy. The optional blanch step locks in a vivid green color that holds for days instead of browning.

Ingredients

  • 3 packed cups fresh basil leaves (~3 oz / 85g)
  • 1/3 cup pine nuts
  • 2 medium cloves garlic, peeled
  • 1/2 cup extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1/2 cup finely grated Parmigiano-Reggiano
  • 1/4 cup finely grated Pecorino Romano
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Pinch of black pepper

Steps

  1. Toast pine nuts in a dry skillet over medium-low heat, stirring frequently, 3-4 minutes until golden and fragrant. Transfer to a plate to cool.
  2. (Optional) Blanch basil in boiling water for 10 seconds, then transfer to ice water. Squeeze dry in a clean towel. This locks in vivid green color.
  3. Add garlic and cooled pine nuts to the food processor. Pulse 8-10 times until coarsely chopped.
  4. Add basil leaves and 1/2 tsp salt. Pulse 10-15 times until broken down but still slightly textured — don't purée.
  5. With the motor running continuously, pour olive oil in a slow, steady stream through the feed tube. Process just until combined, about 15 seconds.
  6. Transfer to a bowl. Fold in the parmigiano and pecorino by hand with a spatula. (Processing the cheese in the machine can make it gummy from the heat.)
  7. Taste and adjust salt.
Notes: Might benefit from a squeeze of lemon juice — try it next time.
Serving: Cook 1 lb pasta (trofie, linguine, or gemelli are traditional). Reserve 1 cup pasta water before draining. Toss pasta with pesto, loosening with pasta water a few tablespoons at a time. Never heat the pesto directly in a pan — the basil will turn dark and bitter.
Storage: Fridge: press plastic wrap onto the surface, drizzle olive oil on top, keeps 4-5 days. Freezer: spoon into ice cube trays, freeze solid, transfer to a bag — keeps 3+ months.

Cory's Fresh Salsa

Easy · 15 min About 3 cups
Original / Cory
snackappetizermexicanvegetarianweeknightmake-aheadbatch-cookingsummerfamily-dinnerfood-processor
+
Why This Works: Seeding the Romas removes excess water so you get a thick, chunky salsa instead of soup. The lime juice and hot sauce deliver both acid and heat, which brightens the raw tomatoes and balances the cilantro's sharpness. Blending to your preferred consistency means you control the texture — chunky or smooth.

Ingredients

  • 6-7 Roma tomatoes, seeded
  • 2 jalapeños, seeded and chopped
  • 1/2 cup chopped cilantro
  • ~10 green onions
  • 2 tbsp hot sauce
  • 1 tsp garlic powder (or minced garlic)
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp pepper
  • 2 tbsp lime juice

Steps

  1. Seed and chop the Roma tomatoes.
  2. Seed and chop the jalapeños.
  3. Roughly chop the cilantro and green onions.
  4. Combine tomatoes, jalapeños, cilantro, and green onions in a food processor or blender.
  5. Blend to desired consistency.
  6. Transfer to a bowl.
  7. Mix in hot sauce, garlic powder, salt, pepper, and lime juice.
  8. Taste and adjust seasoning as needed.
  9. Chill before serving for best flavor.

New Orleans Red Beans and Rice

Weekend Project · 6+ hours (plus overnight soak) Serves 6-8
Original / Cory
dinnersoutherncajunporkweekend-projectbatch-cookingmake-aheadwinter-warmerfamily-dinnerdutch-oven
+
Why This Works: Andouille, tasso, and ham hocks layer three different styles of pork smoke and salt into the broth, which the beans absorb over the long simmer. Mashing a portion of the beans partway through creates a creamy, gravy-like body without any added thickener. The butter stirred in at the end rounds out the richness and gives the cooking liquid a velvety finish.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb dry red kidney beans
  • 1/2 lb andouille sausage
  • 4 oz tasso
  • 2 ham hocks
  • 1/2 stick unsalted butter plus one more pad
  • 1 tbsp avocado oil
  • 32 oz low sodium chicken broth
  • 1 red bell pepper
  • 1 green bell pepper
  • 1 yellow onion
  • 2 tbsp fresh parsley
  • Tony's seasoning (4 good back-and-forth shakes)
  • Garlic powder (4 good back-and-forth shakes)
  • Onion powder (4 good back-and-forth shakes)
  • Italian seasoning (2 good shakes)
  • Thyme (1 good shake)
  • 5 bay leaves

Steps

  1. Soak the beans in water overnight in the fridge.
  2. Dice all produce — bell peppers, onion, and parsley.
  3. Slice tasso and andouille into bite-size chunks.
  4. Melt half a stick of butter and 1 tbsp avocado oil in a big pot.
  5. Add sausage and tasso to the pot. Brown for a few minutes, stirring to get all sides.
  6. After a few minutes, add all the diced produce.
  7. Add 4 good back-and-forth shakes of Tony's, garlic powder, and onion powder. Add 2 good shakes of Italian seasoning and 1 good shake of thyme. Add 5 bay leaves.
  8. When produce is translucent, add soaked beans, 32 oz chicken broth, ham hocks, and enough water to fully cover and make it a bit soupy.
  9. Bring to a boil, then slowly reduce to a simmer.
  10. Simmer with the lid on but slightly cracked for ventilation. Stir every 20-30 minutes so it doesn't stick.
  11. After about 4-5 hours, remove ham hocks and mash some beans in the pot to thicken.
  12. Add a pad of butter. Continue simmering for another hour or two.
  13. After mashing and thickening, turn heat down to low (1-2). Beans can stick/burn easily once thick.
  14. Serve over rice with green onion and any other fixings.
Notes: Low and slow — plan for 5-6 hours total cook time.

Taco Meat with Corn Tortillas

Easy · 30 min Serves 4 (8 tacos)
dinnermexicanbeefweeknightfamily-dinnerskillet
+
Why This Works: Using fattier ground beef (70-80% lean) keeps the meat juicy after draining and gives the spice blend something to cling to. The tomato sauce simmers into the meat and acts as a binder that holds the seasoning in a thin glaze rather than dusty dry spices. Shallow-frying the corn tortillas transforms them from floppy and bland into crisp, toasty shells that hold up under toppings.

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ground beef (70-80% lean)
  • 1 tbsp chili powder
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 3/4 tsp cumin
  • 1/2 tsp dried Mexican oregano
  • 1/4 tsp granulated garlic
  • 1/4 tsp granulated onion
  • 1/2 cup tomato sauce
  • 8 white corn tortillas
  • 1/2 cup avocado or vegetable oil

Steps

  1. Heat a large skillet over medium heat. Add the ground beef, breaking it up with a wooden spoon. Cook fully until browned and no longer pink.
  2. Drain any excess grease from the skillet. Return to stove and reduce heat to low.
  3. Add the tomato sauce, chili powder, salt, cumin, oregano, granulated garlic, and granulated onion. Stir until the meat is coated in the sauce.
  4. Simmer for 5 minutes.
  5. Pour 1/2 cup oil in a medium skillet and heat over medium-high. Test by dipping a tortilla edge — if it sizzles and bubbles, it's ready.
  6. Gently lay a tortilla in the oil and fry each side for about 30 seconds, just enough to add color and crispness.
  7. Remove to a paper towel and carefully fold the tortilla over into a taco shape using tongs or two forks. Repeat with remaining tortillas.
  8. Fill tortillas with taco meat and add desired toppings.
Notes: Use 70-80% lean ground beef for best flavor. Mexican oregano is key — it's different from Italian oregano.

Vodka Sauce with Sausage

Easy · 30 min Serves 4
dinneritalianporkweeknightone-panfamily-dinnerdate-nightskillet
+
Why This Works: The vodka dissolves flavor compounds in the tomato paste that are alcohol-soluble but not water- or fat-soluble, unlocking a fuller tomato taste you can't get otherwise. Browning the sausage hard before building the sauce creates a fond that deglazes into the vodka, adding meaty depth. The heavy cream emulsifies with the tomato paste to create a stable, silky sauce that clings to penne.

Ingredients

  • 8 oz uncooked penne pasta
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 16 oz Italian sausage
  • 1/2 small onion, chopped finely
  • 1 clove garlic, minced
  • 1/3 cup vodka
  • 1/3 cup tomato paste
  • 1 cup heavy/whipping cream
  • Salt and pepper to taste
  • Fresh basil, sliced thin (optional)
  • Freshly grated parmesan cheese (optional)

Steps

  1. Boil a generously salted pot of water and cook the penne al dente according to package directions.
  2. Meanwhile, add butter, sausage meat, and onions to a skillet over medium-high heat. Cook 7-10 minutes until nicely browned, breaking the sausage up as you go. Drain excess fat.
  3. Stir in the garlic and vodka. Let the sauce bubble for about 30 seconds.
  4. Stir in the tomato paste and cream until you have a smooth mixture.
  5. Reduce heat to medium-low. Let the sauce warm through and thicken to your liking.
  6. Season with salt and pepper. Stir in fresh basil if using. Toss with drained pasta, thinning with a splash of pasta water if needed. Serve with freshly grated parmesan.
Notes: Use double-concentrated tomato paste (Mutti or DeLallo). Don't sub the heavy cream for milk or half-and-half — it'll curdle. Buy bulk Italian sausage or remove from casings.
